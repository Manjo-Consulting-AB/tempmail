# New hosting provider, new mail pipeline

We just switched hosting providers, and took the opportunity to redo something I'd been thinking about for a long time: how mail actually gets into TempMail.

At our previous hosting provider, access was "fairly" limited, which meant we had to poll a catch-all address over IMAP every 5 minutes. Now we receive mail directly via STDIN the moment it lands on the server. It might sound like a detail, but it's actually a fairly big architecture upgrade under the hood, and it shows up mainly in three places: latency, disk load, and how evenly the server works.

### Latency and waiting

This is the part I've cared about the most, since it's the one that's visible and felt. Many of you sit and wait for a verification code when using a TempMail address, and sitting there refreshing the inbox while the seconds tick by isn't fun for anyone, even though you can force a check of the inbox.

Before, it took between 0 and 300 seconds for an email to show up. On average, that's 2.5 minutes — long enough to grab a cup of coffee. That was simply because we waited for the next polling cycle before the email was even picked up.

Now the time it takes for an email to land in the database has been squeezed down to just under 0.5 seconds (0.374 seconds on average, to be precise) after the mail server receives it. That's a reduction of more than 600x on a good day! It changes the whole feel of the service.

### Less disk, more memory

The old path was long. Exim wrote the email to disk as a Maildir file, Dovecot read it from there over IMAP, the file was updated again when the email was removed or marked, and finally the same data was written to the database. Four disk operations for one and the same email. Imagine the detour needed just to "trick" a hosting provider.

Now the email is streamed directly in memory via `php://stdin` from Exim to the script that handles it, and is only written once, directly to the database. That gives around 80-90% fewer disk I/O operations. Less waiting on disk, and significantly fewer things that can go wrong along the way.

### No more IMAP to wait on

Every 5 minutes we used to run a cron job that would open a TCP connection, do a TLS handshake, authenticate against the mail server, fetch a list of new emails, and send a series of IMAP commands. All of that just to check whether anything new had arrived.

Now zero IMAP connections are made. When an email arrives, Exim forks a lightweight process that parses the email and exits again immediately. No handshake, no authentication, nothing to wait for.

### More even load

This is maybe the change I like the most purely aesthetically, even though nobody but me saw it. Before, the load looked like a series of spikes — every 5 minutes the server would suddenly have to handle a whole batch of new emails at once. Now it's event-driven: the server only works the milliseconds an email actually comes in, and otherwise sits idle.

No drama, no queues building up, just a steady stream of small, fast jobs.

### Why this matters

TempMail is fundamentally a service where speed is the whole point. Nobody loads a throwaway address to sit and wait for several minutes. This change of hosting provider gave us the chance to redo the mail flow from the ground up, and the result is a service that feels almost instantaneous instead of feeling like waiting for a bus.

### Another bonus: better spam protection
Previously, all emails landed in a catch-all mailbox regardless of whether the recipient address was active or not. The sender therefore always got the impression that the address existed. That meant spammers kept sending emails to addresses that had long since expired, forcing our system to needlessly receive and sort spam.

Now we instead create dynamic forwarders for each active address. If an email is sent to an address that has been shut down, it's rejected immediately by Exim during the SMTP handshake with a genuine SMTP 550 ("User unknown").

The email is never received, the script doesn't need to run, no backscatter is created — and the sender gets a clear signal to stop sending more mail here.

In short: same TempMail, same addresses. Just significantly faster, cleaner, and a little cooler on the inside!
