# RSS feeds, one address at a time

I’ve always liked RSS for the same reason I like Mail Shield: it lets me decide when I want to consume information.

Until now, Mail Shield’s RSS feed was account-wide. That worked well, but it also meant that everything arriving in my Mail Shield inbox ended up in the same feed.

That has now changed.

## RSS feeds for your sticky addresses

You can now enable an RSS feed for each of your sticky addresses.

That makes it possible to give different kinds of email their own feeds.

For example, you might have:

- `news@…` for newsletters
- `shopping@…` for receipts and offers
- `projects@…` for services and project-related mail

Subscribe to a newsletter using the address you created for newsletters, enable its RSS feed, and that content stays in that feed.

No other Mail Shield mail gets mixed in.

The same thing works the other way around: you can enable RSS on all your addresses and decide in your RSS reader which feeds you actually want to follow.

## I use it as a filter for my attention

This is how I personally use RSS.

Some senders are interesting enough that I want to know about them immediately. I enable notifications for those feeds in my RSS reader.

Everything else can wait.

There’s no reason a newsletter arriving at 14:03 should interrupt whatever I’m doing just because it technically arrived in my inbox. I’d rather see it when I have time to read it.

So I have a few feeds that can notify me immediately, while the rest are things I simply scroll through every now and then — often once a week.

That gives me a useful split:

**Things I care about now → notifications.**

**Things I care about eventually → RSS.**

And everything still stays separate from my primary inbox.

## Email doesn't have to behave like email

This is really what I like about the per-address feeds.

An email address doesn't have to mean *another inbox I need to monitor*.

It can simply be an input into whatever workflow makes sense for you.

One address can feed an RSS reader. Another can be used for a service you don't fully trust yet. Another can collect newsletters. They all end up in Mail Shield, but you decide where the information goes next.

The new per-address RSS feeds make that separation a little more useful.

### Getting started

Create or use one of your sticky addresses, then enable **RSS** for it. Mail Shield will give that address its own private feed that you can add to your favourite RSS reader.

You can keep using the account-wide feed as before, or use individual feeds when you want more control over what ends up together.

Sometimes the best way to deal with information overload isn't to process information faster.

It's to stop mixing everything together in the first place.
